# N.U SEO and Media Publication Protocol
**Protocol ID:** NU-SEO-001  
**Version:** 1.0.1  
**Status:** PUBLIC DEVELOPMENT STANDARD  
**Audience:** N.U SEO team, article writers, editors, independent publishers, browser users, and AI generators.

## 1. Purpose
Make useful N.U articles discoverable by people and search systems while protecting accuracy, source context, creator independence, and the distinction between machine validation and human decisions.

This protocol is usable without access to N.U private infrastructure. A writer may give the public protocol to any capable AI generator, use its output as a draft, and complete the applicable checks with available tools.

## 2. SEO is useful discovery, not ranking manipulation
The goal is to answer a real searcher's question with original, helpful, well-sourced content. Do not promise first-page ranking, guaranteed indexing, AI citations, traffic, or search-engine approval. Do not use keyword stuffing, copied competitor text, doorway pages, fake reviews, fabricated authorship, link schemes, or mass-produced pages with no distinct value.

Use natural language, meaningful structure, and a clear answer to the reader's intent. SEO, answer-engine discovery, and AI retrieval share many fundamentals: accessible content, clear entities, accurate statements, useful context, crawlable links, and trustworthy provenance. No special phrase guarantees inclusion in an AI answer.

## 3. Required article workflow
1. **Define intent:** identify the actual question, audience, language, and task the article should satisfy.
2. **Choose a primary topic:** one main subject per article. Add related terms only when they clarify the subject.
3. **Retrieve N.U sources:** search the public N.U index, public source works, and relevant public protocols before drafting. Do not invent missing N.U definitions.
4. **Research external facts:** where the article makes current, scientific, legal, technical, or other factual claims, inspect appropriate primary and reliable sources. Record dates and limitations.
5. **Map claims:** label N.U source statements, external facts, interpretation, supported inference, proposed development, speculation, and unresolved conflict.
6. **Create an original article:** answer the reader's question early; then explain context, evidence, examples, implications, and limitations.
7. **Structure for humans and crawlers:** use one clear title, a descriptive opening, meaningful H2/H3 headings, short paragraphs where appropriate, lists/tables only when useful, descriptive links, and a concise conclusion.
8. **Prepare page metadata:** where the publishing platform supports it, write a unique title tag, concise meta description, readable URL slug, canonical URL, language declaration, and social preview. Do not claim a platform field was set if it was not available.
9. **Add meaningful internal links:** connect to the original N.U source, relevant public protocol, relevant bulletin, and related published works. Each linked page should add context; do not create artificial link loops.
10. **Check media accessibility:** write useful alt text for meaningful images; use captions and transcripts where relevant; do not put essential information only inside an image.
11. **Machine validate:** run the applicable N.U content, source, link, structure, and claim checks. Report what was actually checked.
12. **Producer review:** the producer reviews the complete article, its factual claims, originality, credits, images, rights, and destination, then decides whether to publish independently or request N.U team review.
13. **N.U team review:** a separate authorized N.U decision may accept or decline the work as an N.U reference/publication. The exact decision and version are recorded publicly when disclosure is authorized.
14. **Publish and index:** after the relevant publisher's own publication decision, add a verified public URL to the correct board. Update the public index and cross-links.

## 4. Article quality checklist
- The title accurately describes the article and matches the search intent.
- The opening gives the reader a direct orientation rather than delaying the answer.
- The article contributes original explanation, analysis, evidence, examples, or experience.
- Important factual claims have direct, relevant citations.
- N.U source statements are not misrepresented as external scientific facts.
- Speculation and fictional material are labelled where confusion is possible.
- The article is not a thin rewrite of other search results.
- Headings describe the content that follows.
- Links point to useful, relevant pages and are checked when possible.
- The article is readable on mobile and accessible to the intended audience.
- The author/producer and publication details are accurate.
- The article's status and destination are explicit.

## 6. Search-readable document design
Public protocol and article pages should:
- Use descriptive filenames and titles.
- Start with a clear H1 and a short purpose statement.
- Include stable IDs, version, status, and update date where relevant.
- Use Markdown headings and ordinary text rather than image-only instructions.
- Include direct links to the governing protocol and the correct public status board.
- Use a small machine-readable index (JSON/YAML) when it materially improves discovery.
- Link reciprocal directions: notice → governing protocol; protocol → notice/board.
- Keep public and private content separated.

Public Markdown makes content readable and searchable, but it does not guarantee that a search engine has indexed the page. Monitor indexing and link health only when the relevant platform/tool is available.

## 7. N.U media publication basket
Every SEO article intended as an N.U media publication belongs in the **Media Publications** basket after it has a verified public URL and the listing is safe to disclose. The board is an index of published items, not a raw draft inbox.

The intended media destinations include N.U's Persian-language and other public media outlets, including the names **N.U Persian Land** and **G.G Persian Land** as supplied by the project. Their exact public URLs must be registered and verified before a link is published; do not guess or substitute a similarly named site.

Board: [Media Publications](https://github.com/niroouniverse-creator/NU-PUBLIC-BULLETINS/blob/main/boards/media-publications.md).

## 8. Separate destination baskets
- Published SEO/media article → Media Publications.
- Independent story, comic, illustration, or creator experiment → Independent Stories.
- Official public N.U reference → N.U Public References, only with a verified public source and correct status.
- General public notices → Announcements.
- Private drafts, raw submissions, personal data, confidential material, and unreviewed sensitive content do not go on public boards.

## 9. Where the writer checks for a response
The protocol itself does not promise an unverified submission channel. Until an authorized public submission route is announced, the creator retains their draft and checks:
- [N.U Public Announcements](https://github.com/niroouniverse-creator/NU-PUBLIC-BULLETINS/blob/main/boards/announcements.md) for process changes and general decisions.
- [Media Publications](https://github.com/niroouniverse-creator/NU-PUBLIC-BULLETINS/blob/main/boards/media-publications.md) for published SEO/media articles and their recorded status.

## 10. External SEO reference baseline

This protocol follows current public search guidance rather than promising ranking outcomes:
- Google recommends helpful, reliable, people-first content, original value, clear authorship, and useful structure: [Creating Helpful, Reliable, People-First Content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) and [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).
- Google identifies keyword stuffing, link spam, and scaled content abuse—including mass-produced AI pages without added value—as spam practices: [Spam Policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).
- For Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, the same foundational SEO practices apply; there are no additional special optimization requirements, and eligibility does not guarantee crawling, indexing, or display: [AI Features and Your Website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

The N.U SEO team should use Search Console or platform analytics only when authorized access exists, and should distinguish observed impressions/clicks from assumptions about ranking or AI retrieval.

## 11. Machine and human decisions are separate
The machine only reports compliance with the published standard and the checks it actually ran. Producer review and N.U team review are separate human decisions. They must not be fabricated, inferred from a machine result, or treated as machine validation inputs.

Both human decisions must be made publicly traceable when authorized: the producer's own publication decision and the N.U team's final decision. They may be recorded in one public notice when both are complete, or in separate dated notices when they occur at different times. Post the decision notice to [N.U Public Announcements](https://github.com/niroouniverse-creator/NU-PUBLIC-BULLETINS/blob/main/boards/announcements.md), linking to this protocol and the relevant output basket. After a media article is actually published, add its verified URL to [Media Publications](https://github.com/niroouniverse-creator/NU-PUBLIC-BULLETINS/blob/main/boards/media-publications.md). If it remains an independent creator publication, route it to [Independent Stories](https://github.com/niroouniverse-creator/NU-PUBLIC-BULLETINS/blob/main/boards/independent-stories.md).

The machine validator does not wait for, require, read, or infer either human decision to complete its machine-validation report. It must return the machine result independently and label human decisions as NOT_RECORDED until a public human record exists.

See the [Creator Review, Status and Recognition Protocol](NU-CREATOR-STATUS-AND-RECOGNITION.md).
